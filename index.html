SignGPT: Toward LLM-Mediated Sign Language Interaction through Gloss-Free Translation and Generation

A unified pose-based framework for bidirectional sign language understanding, generation, and exploratory sign-to-sign interaction.

About the paper

One model for sign language translation, generation, and conversation

Pose-based · Gloss-free

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) offer limited support for sign languages, which constrains interaction through signing. We present SignGPT, a unified, pose-based framework for sign language translation and generation, and further explore a sign-to-sign conversational response pipeline. SignGPT couples hierarchical representations of body, hand, and facial motion with a shared language model that produces both text and motion without relying on gloss annotations. Asymmetric multi-token prediction and progressive training together enable bidirectional, multitask interaction. We evaluate SignGPT on How2Sign (American Sign Language, ASL) and PHOENIX14T (German Sign Language, DGS) through quantitative and qualitative analyses as well as ablation studies. An exploratory study with 12 Deaf ASL signers provides descriptive ratings of response appropriateness and motion smoothness. Our findings surface both the opportunities and the limitations of pose-based, LLM-mediated sign language interaction, and provide a foundation for developing and studying conversational interfaces that support sign languages.
